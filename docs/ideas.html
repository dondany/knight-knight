Why more content

The story starts thin

Story begins with the Medieval army alone: five units, and the first turn offers nothing but Peasants. A second tier-1 unit per army, plus cheap mercenaries for hire from the start, gives turn 1 three different recruits instead of one.

Armies skip tiers

Every army has five units and each one is missing a tier, so some shop tiers add nothing for a single-army player. Three more units per army closes every gap from tier 1 to tier 6.

More lands to conquer

Six lands is a short campaign. Each new army below comes with a land, a champion and a bonus that plays unlike the existing six.

Three more units per army

Each army gets a second tier-1 unit, a mid-tier unit, and whatever tier it is missing. Mercenaries grow from two units to a full line of five. "Needs" says whether the ability works with the triggers the simulator already has.

Also considered

  • Zulu, "Horns of the Buffalo": your front and rearmost units are strengthened, rewarding a line with two anchors.

  • Ottomans, "Janissary Volley": the first clash of the battle is preceded by a gunpowder volley from every unit behind the front.

Cost and order

Art

The 21 extra units need 21 new 16×16 sprites: about four more rows on the sheet. The five armies need 25 more, plus a battlefield and a map region each. Code for most units is one def(...) line.

A risk: the recruit pool

Skirmish would go from 32 units to 53, then 78. Recruits are drawn evenly from everything unlocked, so copies get rarer and merging gets harder with every army added. More content should ship together with army packs or weighted shop odds.

Suggested order

  1. Medieval and Mercenary units (6 sprites). This alone fixes the thin story start.
  2. The other 15 units. Every army covers every tier. Rebalance with the simulator.
  3. Persians and Carthaginians. Both sit in the part of the map that already exists, and both bonuses are small simulator changes.
  4. Han and Mongols. They fill the long empty road to Kyoto. Mongols need the most new battle code.
  5. Aztecs. Last, because the bonus needs battles to report back to the shop and the map needs a sea route.

Looked at and left out

  • Grid positioning (Teamfight Tactics, Dota Underlords, Mechabellum). The single line is what keeps a battle readable at 320×180.
  • Shared drafts and carousels. They need players online at the same time.
  • Inventory puzzles (Backpack Battles). A different game.
